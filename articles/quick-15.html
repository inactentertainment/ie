    AI content needs a reason to exist

    Google’s current guidance puts usefulness and review ahead of volume.

    InAct Entertainment Editorial · Updated October 9, 2026
    250 words · 2 min read · Practical quick read

    Back to articles

    Start with the idea

    Start with the idea

    Google’s generative AI content guidance, updated October 1, 2026, stresses accuracy, quality, relevance, and added value. It also warns that scaled content created without value may violate spam policies. The practical message for a small business is to judge the usefulness of the finished page, not the speed of producing it.

    Begin with a real question from your audience. A gardener may need to compare bed layouts. A coach may need a practice sequence. A new listener may want to understand an AI artist identity. Those questions provide a reason for the article and a standard for deciding what belongs in it.

    AI can help organize notes or propose an outline, but the final page should contain your decisions. Add a specific example, a useful activity, or a tool the reader can apply. Check facts against dependable sources and separate a current announcement from your interpretation of what it might mean.

    Review the title and description as carefully as the body. Do they promise more than the article delivers? Are they clear about the audience and task? A polished headline that attracts the wrong visitor can produce frustration instead of a useful relationship.

    Publish at a pace that leaves room for maintenance. Store review dates and reference links so you can revisit changing claims. Remove obsolete guidance or explain the earlier context when it remains relevant. A small collection of purposeful lessons can create more lasting value than a large collection of interchangeable pages that nobody has time to verify.
