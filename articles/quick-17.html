    Measure the route, not just the click

    Use consistent campaign links and a clear outcome.

    InAct Entertainment Editorial · Updated October 9, 2026
    241 words · 2 min read · Practical quick read

    Back to articles
    Your learning route
    Start with the idea

    Start with the idea

    A click tells you someone moved to another page. It does not tell you whether the destination helped them. Before promoting a resource, define the outcome you care about: hearing a release, opening a free tool, downloading a worksheet, or making a qualified service inquiry.

    Google Analytics documentation describes campaign parameters such as utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign. They can identify where a visit came from and which campaign directed it. Use a consistent naming plan so the same source does not appear under several accidental spellings.

    For a small studio, start with a simple tracking sheet. Record the campaign name, destination, audience, date, and intended action. Add the exact link used in each placement. If a social post and an email both promote the same resource, distinguish their source while keeping the campaign name consistent.

    Review the whole journey. A campaign may produce many visits because its message is broad, while few visitors find the tool they expected. Another may produce fewer visits but more meaningful use. The right interpretation depends on the purpose you defined before publishing the link.

    Keep measurement proportionate to the task. Do not place private personal information in campaign URLs. Use aggregated observations where they answer the question, and explain any analytics choices when publishing the site. A useful report connects the source, destination, and outcome in plain language. It should help you decide what to improve next, rather than simply celebrate the largest number available.
