    Start with the idea

    An experimental artist is more compelling when there is a reason for the experiment. Start with the feeling you want to explore: renewal after a difficult season, the confidence of a late-night drive, or the quiet joy of returning home. That intention gives you something to evaluate beyond whether a generated track sounds polished.

    At InAct, AI music and artist identities can become a creative laboratory for exploring how songs are imagined, presented, and experienced. Describe a persona as a fictional, AI-assisted artistic identity. Give listeners the story of the project without inventing a real biography, lived experience, or human performer behind a generated voice.

    DistroKid accepts music made with AI tools, subject to its requirements around rights, impersonation, infringement, and spam. Acceptance by a distributor does not replace your own review. Keep a record of the tools, assets, permissions, and human edits involved in a release.

    Write a short creative brief before generating anything. Name the emotional arc, lyrical perspective, instrumentation, and one distinctive musical constraint. Then compare different versions against the brief. A better chorus, a more deliberate pause, or a less crowded arrangement may matter more than another round of generation.

    Transparency can be part of the artistic language. Invite the audience into the experiment: explain what you directed, what the technology contributed, and what you are still learning. That makes the project an evolving relationship with listeners rather than a mysterious identity they must decode.
