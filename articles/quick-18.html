    Use AI to prepare a better email, then edit it

    Give the draft a purpose and keep the final voice human.

    InAct Entertainment Editorial · Updated October 9, 2026
    249 words · 2 min read · Practical quick read

    Back to articles
    Your learning route
    Start with the idea

    Start with the idea

    A good email has a reason to reach the reader today. It might announce a new lesson, share a useful tool, or invite a customer to take one next step. AI can help arrange the message, but it should not decide why the relationship matters or what the business is prepared to promise.

    Prepare a brief with the audience, purpose, approved facts, desired action, and tone. Include the real destination link and any condition the reader needs to know, such as an email requirement for a free resource. Ask for a draft based on that material rather than an invented promotional story.

    Edit the opening first. Remove vague excitement and explain the benefit in concrete terms. Then check each claim, especially deadlines, prices, tool capabilities, and words such as “free.” Make the action easy to locate. A reader should not need to decode several competing requests in the same message.

    Read the email aloud. Does it sound like a person from your business? Would you make the same promise in a conversation? Replace stiff phrasing and exaggerated urgency with language you can stand behind. A shorter message may be more effective if it preserves the useful information.

    HubSpot Academy offers free email marketing training with an account. Use formal lessons to strengthen your understanding, then apply what you learn to a small, relevant message. Review audience response and confusion after sending. The useful experiment is whether the communication helps people act, not whether AI can produce more emails faster.
