    Start with the idea

    A repeatable release workflow protects your attention. During exploration, you can try unusual arrangements, alternate lyrics, and different artistic directions. During publication, you need a stable set of files and decisions. Mixing those stages can leave you rewriting a song while also trying to remember which cover belongs to it.

    Divide the project into five stages: intention, experimentation, selection, review, and release. Give each stage an output. Intention produces a brief; experimentation produces candidates; selection produces the chosen arrangement; review produces a checked package; release produces the public listening page and promotional material.

    Use one release sheet with the title, artist identity, version name, lyrics, artwork, contributors, permissions, and destination links. Record what remains unresolved. If a field is uncertain, make that visible rather than filling it with a convenient assumption. DistroKid’s AI music guidelines emphasize rights and prohibit impersonation and mass-generated spam, reinforcing the value of deliberate review.

    Create a listening checklist that reflects your project. Can you understand the lyric? Does the ending feel intentional? Is the vocal identity presented honestly? Does the cover remain readable at a small size? Do the links lead to the correct release? Ask another person to test the journey if possible.

    After publication, write a short reflection. Record what took too long, what confused a listener, and what you would repeat. That turns each release into an improvement of the studio’s process. A manageable workflow makes room for experimentation because fewer practical details are left to chance.
