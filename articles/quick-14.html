    Keep a visual website light on its feet

    Treat every logo and banner as part of the loading experience.

    InAct Entertainment Editorial · Updated October 9, 2026
    245 words · 2 min read · Practical quick read

    Back to articles
    Your learning route
    Start with the idea

    Start with the idea

    A portfolio can look restrained and still carry large image files. Logos, decorative backgrounds, and article banners add up, especially when the same asset is repeated in several components. A visitor opening the page from a business card may be using a phone on an uneven connection.

    Make an asset inventory before launch. Record each image’s dimensions, file size, display size, and role. A logo shown at eighty pixels rarely needs a multi-megapixel source in production. Preserve the original artwork separately while preparing an appropriately sized web version for the actual layout.

    Google’s web.dev image-performance material explains the importance of image formats, sizing, and loading choices. Apply those ideas according to the image’s purpose. Keep essential branding available early; defer below-the-fold imagery when appropriate. Specify image dimensions so the page can reserve space while files load.

    SVG artwork is useful for simple article banners and recognizable icons because shapes can stay crisp at different sizes. It is less suitable as a replacement for every photograph or detailed raster logo. Choose the format that serves the artwork rather than forcing everything through one technique.

    Measure the page on a representative phone and connection before declaring it finished. Look for delayed controls, shifting layouts, and images that dominate the transfer size. A portable prototype may embed assets for convenience, while a published version can serve optimized files separately. The goal is to keep the richness of the visual identity while making the first useful interaction arrive quickly.
