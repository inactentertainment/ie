    Give AI drafts a useful review gate

    Replace “looks good” with a few checks tied to the actual task.

    InAct Entertainment Editorial · Updated October 9, 2026
    246 words · 2 min read · Practical quick read

    Back to articles
    Your learning route
    Start with the idea

    Start with the idea

    A review gate is a pause where someone checks whether work is ready to move forward. For an AI-assisted business draft, that pause should focus on the consequences of the message. A polished paragraph can still contain a wrong date, an invented feature, or a promise the business cannot keep.

    Design the review around the task. For a proposal, verify the customer’s goal, scope, price, timeline, and exclusions. For an educational article, verify factual claims and the references supporting them. For a meeting summary, distinguish decisions from suggestions and identify unresolved questions. Different outputs need different checks.

    NIST’s generative AI profile describes risk management across the AI lifecycle. In a small studio, translate that broad approach into a practical routine: identify what could go wrong, decide who checks it, and record the correction when something does go wrong. A short checklist that people actually use is more valuable than an elaborate document they ignore.

    Keep the original input accessible during review. Compare the draft with the approved notes rather than judging it in isolation. Ask whether it preserved uncertainty, omitted something important, or quietly expanded the assignment. Give the reviewer permission to return the draft for clarification.

    Track recurring corrections for a few weeks. If the same mistake appears repeatedly, improve the source material or the drafting instructions. The goal is a workflow that learns from its failures. Human review then becomes a source of better process design, rather than a last-minute attempt to catch everything.
