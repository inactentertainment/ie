# InAct Courses: curriculum and editorial blueprint

Updated October 9, 2026. This replaces the article-first publishing structure. Ten ordered course outlines are available, with 60 modules and 120 planned lesson topics. Five deeper lessons are published. The planned topics are assignments, not completed lessons. The short quick reads have been retired.

## Learning promise

Adult subjects in plain language, with a target of seventh- to eighth-grade reading clarity. Explain a new term before relying on it. Do not speak down to adults. Write as a thoughtful teacher: use everyday examples, active verbs, and clear reasons. Depth comes from examples, evidence, and practice rather than hard words. Existing deeper lessons have new introductory primers; their full bodies still need a plain-language revision before claiming a measured reading level.

Learners may follow the suggested order or enter a course that fits their immediate need. Each course moves from basic concepts to one useful project. The paths overlap by design: a business offer becomes a marketing example; a workflow becomes a web app; a music project becomes a release and audience plan.

## The InAct lesson format

1. Open with a real question and one observable learning outcome.
2. Explain three to five terms in ordinary words. Show why they matter.
3. Teach one idea with a small worked example. Label invented data and hypothetical businesses.
4. Place a guided video where it helps. Attribute the original publisher, state what to notice, and provide a verified start time. Give a fallback publisher link.
5. Ask a short question and explain the reasoning behind the answer.
6. Let the learner try the idea in a small interactive tool, comparison, graph, or decision exercise.
7. Continue through three practice checkpoints of three questions each. Vary the response: choice, scenario decision, matching or selection. Feedback teaches rather than merely scoring.
8. Use a second video only when it adds a different useful perspective. Do not pad lessons with embeds.
9. Finish with a real application task and a short assessment. Use silver celebration, green confirmation, and useful red correction. A passing quiz is not a professional qualification.
10. Offer personal notes and a downloadable copy. Describe local browser storage clearly. Do not promise an account or cross-device sync.
11. Include primary references with linked author-date citations and APA-style reference entries. Explain where editorial guidance differs from reported facts.

Aim for 3,000–4,000 words in developed major lessons when the topic needs that depth. Foundations may be taught in smaller connected steps, but do not revive unrelated quick reads. Alternate explanation with visual blocks, worked examples, reflection, and practice. Use animated SVG banners and diagrams, restrained motion, reduced-motion support, platinum practice cards, and the consistent InAct header and music player. Keep affiliate placements visually designed; disclose an actual affiliate relationship when a link is installed. Advertising must not be mistaken for evidence or an endorsement from a speaker.

Course projects should eventually use a simple rubric: clarity, evidence, and usefulness, each rated 0–2 with an explanation and a revision task. Do not mark a course complete just because a video played. Learning means being able to explain, apply, and check the idea.

   - Choose a meaningful problem (planned)

### 04. Start & Grow a Small Business

### 05. Funding Your Business

Learn the options, costs, documents, and questions behind business funding.

Project: Prepare a funding-readiness checklist and compare options using verified terms.

1. Understand the need

   - Separate startup costs, working capital, and equipment (planned)

2. Know the options
   - Self-funding, loans, credit lines, and equity (planned)
   - SBA programs, community lenders, and grant limitations (planned)

3. Read the terms
   - Interest, fees, repayment, collateral, and guarantees (planned)
   - Compare total cost and the effect on cash flow (planned)

4. Prepare the evidence
   - Business plans, financial statements, and projections (planned)
   - Organize records and explain assumptions (planned)

5. Use AI for preparation
   - Create a document checklist and draft questions (planned)
   - Verify providers, protect sensitive data, and review every claim (planned)

6. Your funding-readiness brief
   - Compare two options with verified figures (planned)
   - Prepare for a lender or adviser conversation (planned)

## Extended Learning

Technology news belongs beside the courses as Extended Learning. Each future update should include the event date, source, what changed, what remains uncertain, a linked relevant course, and one practical action. Keep historical demonstrations labeled with their year. Do not claim a video from 2023 explains every current product. Avoid unsupported viral rankings, predictions presented as fact, or an extra speculative doom category.
