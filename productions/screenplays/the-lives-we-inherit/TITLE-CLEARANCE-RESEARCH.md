**Scope:** Preliminary public-web screening and review of official U.S. copyright / trademark guidance; *not* a legal opinion or clearance certificate.

## Public-web matches and context

1. **Exact-title publication:** *The Lives We Inherit* is used by an existing Substack publication describing itself as a feminist history newsletter, pairing historical women's lives with contemporary questions. https://thelivesweinherit.substack.com/about
2. **Associated independent bookshop/profile:** `https://uk.bookshop.org/shop/tlwi` appears under *The Lives We Inherit* and links to the Substack account. This may be the same operator, not a second independent claimant.
3. **Similar phrase in other titles:** *All My Winters Lead to You: The Love We Find in the Lives We Inherit* appears in an online bookseller listing. This is a longer title, not evidence of an exclusive right to the exact proposed title.
4. Other instances of the phrase appear in article text, essays and similar expressions. None, by itself, establishes an enforceable trademark conflict.

**What this search does NOT establish:** No determination has been made whether any federal or state trademark registration is live, whether the newsletter uses the phrase as a protected source-identifying mark, or whether theatrical/television title-conflict rules, international rights or common-law rights affect production use. The live interactive USPTO database has not been exhaustively searched in this research.

## Relevant official law and practice

- **Copyright:** The U.S. Copyright Office says titles, names and short phrases are not copyrightable, though their creative works may be. https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf
- **Single creative work title:** The USPTO generally refuses to register the title of just one creative work as a mark; a series title may qualify depending on use and evidence. https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/laws/title-single-work-refusal-and-how-overcome-refusal
- **Likelihood of confusion:** Similar marks can create conflict when goods or services are related and consumers may believe they share a source. https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion
- **Full searches:** USPTO guidance recommends a comprehensive clearance search, including variations, registrations/applications and evidence beyond the federal database. https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching

## Recommended clearance steps, before external commercial submission

- [ ] Conduct a live USPTO Trademark Search for exact wording, reordered wording, approximate wording and partial marks in relevant entertainment, audiovisual and publishing services; inspect status, owners, dates and classes, not just search-result titles. https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/
- [ ] Search relevant state trademark records, common-law uses, IMDb/film-and-series catalogs, publishers/book databases and international markets where distribution is planned.
- [ ] Investigate the existing Substack title, its operator and first-use history, and any connection to entertainment-related services.
- [ ] Have entertainment/IP counsel assess conflicts, television-title clearance and chain of title for the specific series.
- [ ] Confirm ownership and release/clearance of scripts, artwork, music, characters and archival sources separately; a title check does **not** clear other materials.
- [ ] Re-check near the time of pitching and prior to signing any distribution agreement, since uses and filings may change.

## Decision

**Retain *The Lives We Inherit* as the working title, but do not label it legally cleared or exclusive.** Do not file a trademark application or approach an existing title user based solely on this preliminary research. No creative material or project name needs to change at this stage.
