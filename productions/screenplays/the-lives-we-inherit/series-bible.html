Series Bible

A producer-facing development bible · Six-part limited series · Working narrative architecture

01 · Series identity

Format: Six-episode television limited series; approximately 55–60 minutes per episode; definitive ending.

Genre: Contemporary family drama, historical mystery and psychological suspense, with moments of humor and a restrained sense of the unexplained.

Logline: A son begs his family to believe him. A mother visits a child who does not know her name. Almost two centuries separate them. The truth will not.

Audience promise: The connections between seemingly unrelated people across nearly two centuries will be revealed through human choices rather than coincidence alone.

02 · The dramatic premise

The Premise

A young man is certain that something is happening to him. His family can see his fear, but they cannot agree on what it means or how to help. Every attempt to protect him seems to push someone else away. For the people who love him, the question is no longer simply what is real. It is whether they can remain a family when they no longer trust one another's understanding of reality. His struggle is his own, not a puzzle or a supernatural spectacle. Yet around him, an older and equally urgent question begins to take shape: How much of what we call our lives began long before we were born?

The Scene That Stays With You

A little girl sits still as a familiar visitor brushes her hair. The woman knows where the child's scalp is tender. She knows how tightly to braid. They say little. There is no reason to suspect that this ordinary tenderness conceals anything at all. The visitor leaves, and the child returns to the people she calls family. Only later will we understand what that woman could not say, and why she kept coming back. By then, the audience has already felt the love before learning its history.

Across Two Centuries

In 1824, a white slaveholder faces financial collapse. For him, ruin means the loss of property and standing. For the Black people he enslaves, his decisions threaten families, freedom and lives. Their stories belong to them; they are not merely the background to his downfall. As decades pass, children grow up far from their beginnings, some families are formed by blood and others by devotion, and ordinary acts of sacrifice or betrayal become the foundations of futures no one can foresee. Each era unfolds as a living story, not a history lesson.

A Family in Motion

In the present, the family cannot afford to stand still while the past reveals itself. A parent's devotion collides with a child's need to be heard. A son's ambitions meet limits he did not choose. A daughter demands room to define her own future. An older woman's memories and uncanny experiences unsettle what others believe they know. Around the hardest moments are jokes told at the wrong time, meals, music, small victories and the daily rituals that make people worth fighting for. Love does not erase what has happened. It makes the choices that follow matter more.

The Mystery and the Promise

At first, these lives appear unrelated. Then a gesture repeats, a name takes on another meaning, and a moment we thought we understood becomes something else entirely. Some connections are ancestral. Others were made by people who chose to stay, leave, protect, conceal or love. With each revelation, the mystery grows more personal. Can this family face what it has inherited without passing the same wounds forward? The answer arrives not through one final secret, but through choices the living must make for themselves.

The Series

The Lives We Inherit is an original six-episode television limited series of approximately 55–60 minutes per episode. It combines intimate contemporary family drama with historical mystery, psychological suspense, humor and a restrained sense of the unexplained. The narrative closes with a definitive, emotionally earned ending. Its final image belongs to the future.

03 · Theme and dramatic argument

Inheritance is more than biology

Bloodlines matter, but the series also recognizes the influence of chosen kinship, withheld truths, adopted families, material circumstances, memory and social power.

Protection and harm

People may act out of love and still cause injury. The narrative tests whether a family can tell the truth while preserving the dignity of the people involved.

Legacy and agency

The past explains pressures but does not eliminate present responsibility. The ending is earned through decisions made by the living.

History without exposition

Historical episodes must stand as immediate human dramas. Their characters are not clues or background material for a present-day mystery.

04 · Narrative architecture

Primary present-day throughline

The contemporary family's crisis is immediate, emotionally specific and continuous. Each episode must advance a concrete decision or consequence in this family while revealing a new dimension of the long historical story.

Interwoven historical movements

A nineteenth-century rupture, later questions of separation and belonging, and twentieth-century paths of ambition, love and loss speak to one another through action, sound, objects, gesture and point of view.

Revelation rules

  • Show the emotional event before explaining its historical connection.
  • Make every timeline legible on entry without overusing dates or title cards.
  • Each revelation should reframe an earlier scene without contradicting what the audience witnessed.
  • Do not use mental distress as a supernatural gimmick or turn illness into a mystery prop.
  • Resolve the series through action and relationship, not solely a surprise family-tree disclosure.

06 · Historical worlds

1820s — An Uneasy Beginning

An early nineteenth-century story involving a financially ruined slaveholder and the lives shaped by his choices. Historical context and dramatic details require further research.

Turn of the Twentieth Century — Belonging

An adoption and family-separation thread explores parenthood, racial identity, longing, and the meanings of home.

Mid-to-Late Twentieth Century — Change

Ambition, athletic promise, work, love, loss and family transformation connect generations.

Present Day — The Living Consequences

A contemporary family navigates relationships, illness, memory and futures yet to be determined.

Historical standards

Research must corroborate the setting, legal and social conditions, geography, material life and historical language before period details are treated as final. Fictional composites or dramatizations must not be presented as verified genealogical claims.

07 · Six-episode arc

01 · The Threshold

Open within a young man's disorienting experience before revealing the people around him. Establish the contemporary family's fractured loyalties. A seemingly distant historical encounter introduces a question the present cannot yet answer.

02 · What Was Kept

The family attempts to regain its footing as a historical story of separation and chosen kinship comes into focus. A small, intimate gesture acquires unexpected significance.

03 · In Another Name

Connections deepen between household, community and lineage. A revelation forces the audience to revisit an earlier scene, while a present-day ambition meets a painful obstacle.

04 · The Lives Between

Choices made by people with unequal power ripple outward. The family confronts the difference between the stories people tell about themselves and the lives others remember.

05 · Inheritance

Threads that once appeared unrelated converge. The emotional consequences arrive before the full explanation, pressing each central character toward a decision.

06 · What Remains

The primary mystery reaches its resolution, but the ending rests on human action rather than a final genealogical trick. In the quiet after the storm, a new beginning changes the family's idea of what can be passed on.

08 · Character and story continuity

Continuity ledger

  • Track every timeline, key relationship, age, residence, separation and return.
  • Record what the audience knows, what each character believes and what is objectively true in every episode.
  • Identify the first appearance of recurring gestures, names, objects and music cues.
  • Separate historical evidence, creator-supplied facts and fictional invention.
  • Keep exact cause-and-effect aligned with the six-episode chronology.

Unresolved material requiring screenplay decisions

Final character names, specific dates and locations, the complete familial link map, the order and content of all major reveals, individual ending states and the definitive last scene are not established in the currently published overview. These remain creative-development decisions rather than facts to invent in a pitch document.

09 · Tone, visual grammar and sound

Cinematography

Subjective close-ups, meaningful silence, shifting points of view and visual bridges across generations.

Transitions Through Time

Scenes transition through recurring objects, matching gestures and emotional echoes rather than relying on exposition.

Soundtrack & Original Score

A developing collection of character motifs, instrumental cues and songs designed to support six hours of storytelling.

Lookbook & Proof of Concept

Visual references and an optional short mood reel can demonstrate the intended experience after the writing and rights are ready.

Performance and pacing

Naturalistic speech, purposeful pauses and behavior after the dialogue are central to the intended rhythm. Humor must arise from character and circumstance, not release the story from emotional accountability.

10 · Production approach

Format discipline

The contained six-episode structure should support a complete narrative with a clear final answer. Running time can vary within commissioning constraints.

Visual development

The existing 25 portraits are concept art, not casting suggestions or likeness approvals. A dedicated lookbook and mood reel may be developed only from rights-cleared material.

Historical and cultural consultation

Historical research and appropriate consultation should inform the portrayal of enslavement, family separation, disability and psychological distress. The work should prioritize dignity, accuracy and the agency of represented characters.

11 · Audience and market positioning

Core proposition

An emotionally driven, intergenerational mystery for viewers who value complex family relationships, historical consequences, layered reveals and a conclusion that rewards rewatching.

Producer conversation

The purpose of this document is to make the creative vision, narrative structure and development needs legible. It does not claim a committed cast, production budget, distribution agreement, rights clearance or completed episode scripts.
